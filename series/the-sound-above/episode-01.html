The Core Question: How Did the First Room Come Together?

In this conversation, recorded on-site at the 2011 Software Craftsmanship North America conference, Sergio Pereira and I talk about the origins of Chicago Alt.NET.

Chicago Alt.NET was the very first technical user group I ever attended, and the very first stage where I ever gave a talk. Meeting on the second Wednesday of every month at Redpoint Technologies in the Sears Tower, Sergio created an open, supportive room for developers who wanted to build software with automated testing, continuous integration, and open source libraries at a time when enterprise IT culture was resistant to outside ideas.

What stands out watching this back today is how much the room was about welcoming people who felt isolated in their daily jobs. It was not about corporate certifications or selling tools. It was about sharing what we were learning after hours and giving developers the courage to bring better practices back to their teams on Monday morning.

Looking Back to Look Forward: Carrying Knowledge Across the Crisis

In Isaac Asimov's Foundation, Hari Seldon foresaw that the dark age between civilizational eras could be dramatically shortened if the core knowledge was deliberately gathered and stewarded by practitioners who understood how things actually worked.

As software engineering changes with AI code synthesis and autonomous developer tools, we face an interregnum of understanding: when syntax generation becomes effortless, the foundational muscle memory of debugging, system boundaries, and root-cause analysis risks atrophying.

The Alt.NET community taught us how to evaluate new technologies with an independent eye, how to rely on test suites and verified feedback loops, and how to build local peer support systems that outlast commercial hype cycles. We preserve these conversations not for nostalgia, but as an empirical manual for how human engineers maintain their agency and craft so that when the hype cycle cools, the knowledge to build durable platforms remains unbroken.
