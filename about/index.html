About Mike Hall

Staff Software Engineer focused on platform reliability, architecture, and making complex production systems safe to change.

How I Work With Teams

I work at the seams of software systems: between code and runtime behavior, across distributed services, and between engineering squads and business goals. With more than two decades in the trade, I focus on helping teams move calmly and safely:

  • Meeting teams where they are: Production systems are shaped by real business constraints and shifting deadlines. I don't bring dogma or judgment to legacy code; I look at what the software actually does in production and help find the next safe improvement.
  • Clear, written architecture: I write explicit Architecture Decision Records (ADRs), system cartography maps, and visual diagrams so teams share the same picture and never rely on guesswork.
  • Collaborative pairing and mentorship: I enjoy working directly with engineers, untangling difficult transactional paths together, and building lasting confidence within the squad.
  • Pragmatic simplicity: I prefer simple, standard-library approaches over speculative abstractions. The best architecture is usually the simplest one that solves the real operational problem.

What I Value on an Engineering Team

Software engineering is a shared discipline played over long horizons. Technical decisions matter, but how teams treat each other while doing the work matters even more:

  • Curiosity over blame: When an outage or defect occurs, we investigate the systemic causes, improve the guardrails, and update our telemetry. Blameless post-mortems build resilient systems and confident teams.
  • Fast feedback loops: Deterministic automated tests, fast local builds, and observable runtime traces build developer velocity. When verification is fast and trustworthy, deployments become routine.
  • Generosity with context: Siloed knowledge creates bottlenecks and anxiety. Great engineering cultures are built on pairing, written documentation, and celebrating team growth.
  • Psychological safety and curiosity: High-performing engineering requires the freedom to ask naive questions, explore playful experiments, and admit when something is unclear without fear. When teams feel safe, complexity becomes manageable and craftsmanship becomes fun.

Background & Lineage

My technical background spans early distributed commerce, real-time networking, enterprise platform modernization, and community stewardship:

  • Production Systems: Built and operated high-volume transactional services, from early real-time ticket broker inventory locking to 10-year enterprise monolith cartography and zero-downtime database migrations.
  • Community & Mentorship: Co-founded Software Craftsmanship McHenry County, helped organize regional developer gatherings, and founded Geekfest@OMF and the OpenTelemetry Working Group to support continuous peer learning in large engineering organizations.
  • The Oral History Archive: Founded UGtastic (preserved on this site as the WHOIS Tech Community archive), recording 218 in-depth conversations with software pioneers and working practitioners across fifteen years of the software craftsmanship movement.
