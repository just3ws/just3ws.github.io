The Origin & Purpose of SCMC

In 2009–2010, when mainstream Agile conferences rejected practical software engineering talks for being "too implementation-focused," the Chicago developer community responded by founding Software Craftsmanship North America (SCNA) and organizing local unvarnished practitioner meetups: the Software Craftsmanship Movement Chicago (SCMC).

Organized by Steve Kim, Jim Suchy, Dave Hoover, and a core group of Chicagoland engineers, SCMC served as a monthly unvarnished laboratory where developers shared code, refactoring techniques, language runtimes (Ruby, Clojure, C++, Perl, Java), and automated testing practices.

Synthesized Historical Lessons from SCMC

  1. Grassroots Meetups Acted as Enterprise R&D Labs: SCMC meetings provided the experimental ground where TDD, Sidekiq, ActiveJDBC, and AOP patterns were vetted before being adopted by high-growth startups like Groupon and ActiveCampaign.
  2. Polyglot Runtime Transition: SCMC sessions bridged the transition from legacy .NET/Java monoliths to dynamic Ruby, Clojure, Python, and Node.js environments across Chicagoland.
  3. Community Drives Engineering Leadership: Meetup organizers were the primary architects of the talent pipelines that powered Chicago's 2010–2015 tech surge.
  4. Durable Wisdom Over Hype: Verbatim video/audio archiving preserves fundamental principles—such as Uncle Bob's "Filling the Vessel" and Corey Haines' practice mindset—that are directly applicable to modern AI-augmented development.
