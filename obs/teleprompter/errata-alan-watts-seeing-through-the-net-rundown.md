## Act 1: Framing (Why This Isn't a Hot-Take Reaction)
- Introduce Errata watch-along format: sitting down with a 1969 talk to IBM systems engineers, transcribed locally via `ztranscribe`.
- Explain why "reaction video" feels too loud for Alan Watts. This is a conversation across 57 years about what systems engineers see when abstract models outrun reality.

## Act 2: Holding Up the Net (`00:15:22`)
- **Cue:** Play `00:15:22` to `00:15:49` ("All biological existences wiggle... held up his net").
- **Map to AI Today:** Tokenizers, vector spaces, context windows, and eval benchmarks are our fisherman's net. Useful for measurement, dangerous when mistaken for the territory.

## Act 3: Prickles and Goo (`00:17:28` / `00:18:49`)
- **Cue:** Play `00:17:28` and `00:18:49` ("gooey prickles and prickly goo").
- **Map to AI Today:** Types, tests, state machines, and deterministic validators (`prickles`) paired with probabilistic LLM generation and semantic search (`goo`). Neither works alone.

## Act 4: Spotlight vs. Floodlight & The Gallbladder Specialist (`00:20:26` / `00:23:41`)
- **Cue:** Play `00:20:26` and `00:23:41`.
- **Map to AI Today:** Context windows and chat prompts are intense spotlights that leave neighboring system contracts in the dark. Single-tool hype turns every engineering problem into a prompt problem.

## Act 5: Mechanism vs. Organism (`00:24:32`)
- **Cue:** Play `00:24:32` ("A mechanism is assembled... an organism grows").
- **Map to AI Today:** Snapping together unreviewed generated code vs. cultivating maintainable software with clear boundaries and fast feedback loops.

## Act 6: Eating the Menu (`00:44:54`)
- **Cue:** Play `00:44:54` to `00:45:09` ("we're starving on eating menus").
- **Close:** Generated code volume and synthetic metrics are the menu. Working, observable, human-serving software is the dinner.
