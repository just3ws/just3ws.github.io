## Why younger developers should see the whole timeline

Efforts to make programming more accessible reach back at least to COBOL in 1959. That is different from predicting the disappearance of programmers. If you entered the industry after 2022, the older record helps put today's claims about automated coding in context.

The 67-year span in this title runs from COBOL's beginnings to 2026. It is an interpretive frame, not evidence of an identical prediction repeated throughout that period. The timeline brings together language design, end-user tools, forecasts about code generation, and a hiring announcement. Those are related questions, but they make different claims.

## The machine's reading: incentives, abstraction, and what actually changed

1. **Follow the incentives of the person holding the microphone.** The loudest "coding is dead" claims come from GPU infrastructure vendors (NVIDIA), cloud compute landlords (AWS), frontier model labs raising capital and selling tokens (Anthropic, Stability AI), and enterprise SaaS CEOs pitching Wall Street on margin expansion (Salesforce). They are not neutral labor historians.
2. **The Abstraction Illusion.** Typing syntax was never the hardest part of software engineering. The hard part is translating ambiguous, contradictory human requirements into systems that do not corrupt data, leak secrets, or fail at 3:00 AM. This article's hypothesis is that cheaper code generation can shift the bottleneck toward understanding, integration, and review.
3. **The honest change for younger developers.** Unlike 1990s CASE tools, LLMs can help produce boilerplate and first drafts. A concern for younger developers is whether employers will offer enough opportunities to learn review, debugging, and architectural judgment. The timeline is not a labor-market study and does not establish the size or cause of any hiring change.
4. **Why learning to code matters more, not less.** You cannot safely review, verify, debug, or steer AI-generated code if you do not understand what the machine is actually doing underneath. As Mike Hall wrote in his January 21, 2025 essay [The Power of People, Not Just AI](https://www.just3ws.com/2025/01/21/the-power-of-people-not-just-ai.html): *"At its core, the work we do is still about people coming together to solve complex problems."*

**Syntax keeps dying. Accountability never does.** Learn to code so you can spot when the machine is lying to you.
