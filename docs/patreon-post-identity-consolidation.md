### Post Body (Ready to Copy/Paste)

More than sixteen years ago, on July 13, 2010, I uploaded a six-second clip to YouTube: "Corey Haines tells you what he thinks of shoddy testing" (https://youtu.be/NEppSADDKsY).

Corey was laughing about Twilight while Noel Rappin held up a concrete pillar in the background. It was the early days of Chicago Software Craftsmanship and Software Craftsmanship McHenry County (SCMC). Just friends in a room who cared about code, having fun.

Sixteen years and almost six thousand days have passed since that upload.

When that much time slips past, memory can play tricks on you. You start to doubt your own story. You wonder if you were just a ghost standing in the hallway, looking in through a crack in the door.

The archive is proof. The recordings are proof.
I was really there.
I was really part of it.
I still am.

Over the years, I put out hundreds of videos, interviews, and writing under different banners: UGtastic, WHOIS Tech Community, organizational titles, and project aliases.

If I am honest about why I created so many wrappers, the truth is simple: I went through years of trying not to be myself online.

As someone who is AuDHD, putting my face, my voice, and my real presence out there felt intensely vulnerable. I felt weird. I worried about how I sounded and how I was perceived. Hiding behind an umbrella brand felt much safer. It was a shield.

Right now, that shield is coming down. I am in the middle of consolidating all of my historical archives, channels, and platform work under my core identity: **just3ws**.

The new banners are up here on Patreon and on YouTube. The old handle reservation has been released, and the fourteen-day countdown is ticking toward claiming @just3ws for the main video home. My site and local systems are aligning under that single name.

Stepping out from behind the shield brings real fear. Not just the fear of internet criticism, but the quiet, colder fear of apathy: that insidious doubt that tells you nobody cares, and that your voice will simply vanish into an empty room.

For years, that fear was loud enough to drown out reality.

I do not believe the apathy lie anymore. When I look around honestly at the people who have supported me, encouraged me, and joined me here on Patreon, the warmth and belief are real. The fear used to drown out your voices. Today, I am choosing to listen to you instead.

Someone recently gave me the classic advice: **"Do it scared."**

If you have ever stood on a skateboard at the coping of a vert ramp, you know that feeling in your gut. Your tail is balanced on the pipe, your front foot is hovering over the bolts, and you look down into the drop. Your stomach falls out, your heart thumps in your chest, and your instincts tell you to step back onto the deck.

If you wait for the fear to go away, you will stand on that platform forever. The fear does not leave while you are standing on the edge.

The only way down the transition is to do it scared. You bend your knees, stomp your front foot into the wall, lean all your weight forward into the empty air, and let gravity do what gravity does.

The migration is moving. The coping is behind me. I am leaning in.

Thank you for being here, for supporting calm craftsmanship, and for helping me do this without hiding.
