## Passage record

The story presents Prince Huei's cook cutting up a bullock. The cook explains
that his knife moves through the natural spaces between the joints rather than
forcing its way through bone. His first sight was of whole animals. After
practice, he no longer sees the whole animal as an undifferentiated obstacle,
and his work becomes an attentive response to the animal's structure. The
prince's conclusion turns the demonstration toward a larger question: how to
care for one's life.

The short source quotation below is included for identification, not as a
replacement for the book:

> “I have always devoted myself to Tao.” (Lin Yutang translation, p. 376)

## Names and conventions

The scanned edition uses **Chuangtse**. That spelling is preserved as the
edition's source form. For modern indexing, the normalized name is
**Zhuangzi**, with **Chuang Tzu** and **Chuang-tzu** retained as historical
aliases. The story is commonly indexed in English as **Cook Ding** or **Butcher
Ding**. Its classical Chinese title is **庖丁解牛**.

The edition uses **Tao**. This record keeps *Tao* when describing the scanned
translation and adds *Dao* as the modern pinyin search form. **Wu wei** is a
useful interpretive connection to the passage's unforced, structure-sensitive
action, but it is not presented as wording that appears in this excerpt.

These conventions follow the distinction between a source's historical form
and a normalized catalog form. The [Chinese Text Project source entry](https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=600239)
provides the classical text context, while the [Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy discussion of Chinese ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-chinese/)
identifies the well-known Cook Ding example and its movement through the spaces
of the joints. The [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on the
Zhuangzi](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2018/entries/zhuangzi/)
provides broader authorship and textual context.

## Reading note

The important recovery here is not a slogan about effortless action. It is a
description of practiced perception. The cook's capability comes from long
attention to structure, repeated contact with real material, and knowing where
force is unnecessary. The work is highly skilled, but it does not announce
itself through domination. That is why the passage belongs beside Mike's
engineering archive: it offers a compact image of changing a living system by
finding its grain, making the cut where the structure already permits it, and
leaving the surrounding system intact.

The surrounding work is also hilariously funny. Chuangtse does not treat
wisdom as a solemn performance. He uses exaggeration, reversals, strange
conversations, animals, bodily awkwardness, and ordinary people with unusual
skill to loosen the reader's grip on certainty. The humor is part of the
method. It makes room for a truth that would be resisted if it arrived as an
instruction.
