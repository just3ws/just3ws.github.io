More than sixteen years ago, on July 13, 2010, I uploaded a six-second clip to YouTube: "Corey Haines tells you what he thinks of shoddy testing" (https://youtu.be/NEppSADDKsY).

Corey was laughing about Twilight while Noel Rappin held up a pillar in the background. It was the early days of Chicago Software Craftsmanship and Software Craftsmanship McHenry County (SCMC). Just friends in a room who cared about code, having fun.

Sixteen years and almost six thousand days have passed since that upload. Across that time, I put out videos and projects under a lot of different names: UGtastic, WHOIS Tech Community, organizational umbrellas, and production aliases.

If I am honest about why I kept creating new wrappers, it was because I spent years trying not to be myself online.

As someone who is AuDHD, putting my face, my voice, and my real presence out there felt intensely vulnerable. I felt weird. I worried about how I sounded and how I came across. Hiding behind an umbrella brand felt safe. It gave me a protective shield.

Right now, that shield is coming down. I am in the middle of consolidating all my video channels, archives, and projects under my core personal identity: just3ws.

The old handle reservation has been released, YouTube's 14-day hold timer is ticking down toward the Day 15 claim window for @just3ws, and my site, Patreon, and banners are aligning to that single name.

Stepping out from behind the shield brings real fear. Not just the fear of internet criticism, but the quiet fear of apathy: the doubt that whispers nobody cares and that your voice will just vanish into an empty room.

For years, that fear drowned out reality. But I do not believe the apathy lie anymore. Too many friends, family members, and community peers have given me real encouragement, patience, and support. Today, I am choosing to listen to them instead.

Someone recently gave me the classic advice: "Do it scared."

If you skateboard, you know what that feels like. You stand on the coping at the top of a vert ramp. Your tail is balanced on the pipe, your front foot is hovering over the bolts, and you look straight down the transition. Your stomach drops, your heart thumps, and your instincts tell you to step back onto the deck.

If you wait for the fear to go away, you will stand on that platform forever. The only way down is to do it scared. You bend your knees, stomp your front foot into the wall, lean all your weight forward into empty air, and let gravity do its thing.

The migration is moving. The coping is behind me. I am leaning in.
