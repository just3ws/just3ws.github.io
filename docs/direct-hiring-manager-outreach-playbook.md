# Direct Hiring Manager Outreach Playbook

A high-signal, zero-fluff communication framework for engaging Engineering Directors, VPs of Engineering, CTOs, and internal peer connections.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy: High-Signal Peer Value vs. Job Pleading

Hiring managers receive dozens of generic recruiter inquiries and template applications daily. Most outreach fails because it follows a transactional pattern:

* ❌ *"Hi, I saw you are hiring for a Principal Engineer. I have attached my 4-page resume and would love to get 30 minutes on your calendar this week to discuss how I can help."*

This fails because it creates work for the hiring manager and presumes they have time to evaluate an uncurated resume.

### The High-Signal Principle
High-performing Principal Engineers do not ask for favors; they provide immediate clarity. High-signal outreach treats the hiring manager as an engineering peer:

* ✅ **Acknowledge specific context:** Cite their exact stack, technical challenge, or team expansion.
* ✅ **Provide Level 1 proof:** Deliver a single sentence demonstrating verifiable high-consequence outcomes.
* ✅ **Offer a tailored 1-page artifact:** Provide a link to a tailored Executive Pitch Brief (`exports/briefs/`) rather than an unguided resume attachment.
* ✅ **Low-friction close:** Never demand calendar time in the first message. Give them an easy, low-pressure option to reply if timing aligns.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  THE 4-PART HIGH-SIGNAL OUTREACH FORMULA                |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. CONTEXT HOOK       --> Why you are writing (specific to their stack) |
| 2. VALUE PROPOSITION  --> 1 sentence: scope, craftsmanship, real scale  |
| 3. ARTIFACT LINK      --> 1-page tailored brief on just3ws.com          |
| 4. ZERO-PRESSURE EXIT --> "No response needed if timing is off."        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 5. The 5 Golden Rules of Executive Outreach

### Rule 1: The 75-Word Limit
If an email or LinkedIn message requires scrolling on a mobile phone, response rates drop by 60%. Keep initial messages under 75 words whenever possible. Let the linked 1-page brief carry the deep technical context.

### Rule 2: Never Ask for "15 Minutes on Your Calendar" First
Requesting calendar time places an immediate scheduling burden on a busy executive. Offer value first. When an executive is impressed by your brief, *they* will invite you to a call.

### Rule 3: Frame as an Autonomous IC, Not Management Overhead
Ensure your language signals active technical mastery:
* **Use:** *"hands-on architecture"*, *"system cartography"*, *"decoupling state machines"*, *"pairing with squads"*.
* **Avoid:** *"driving executive strategy"*, *"managing cross-functional stakeholders"*, *"overseeing agile delivery"*.

### Rule 4: Eliminate All AI Jargon
Avoid machine-generated filler words:
* ❌ *"I am an ambitious innovator thrilled to leverage enterprise frameworks..."*
* ✅ *"I specialize in high-consequence Ruby on Rails platforms and zero-downtime database migrations."*

### Rule 5: Zero Em Dashes
Enforce natural, confident human sentence structure. Use periods, colons, or parentheses rather than em dashes to maintain an authentic personal voice.

## 6. The 1-Follow-Up Protocol

If a hiring manager does not respond within five business days, send exactly **one** lightweight follow-up that adds new value:

```markdown
Hi [Name],

Following up briefly on my earlier note. We recently published an architectural retrospective on safely decoupling 10-year-old monoliths and running telemetry-driven database migrations:

👉 https://just3ws.com/2026/08/29/system-cartography-how-to-map-a-ten-year-old-monolith/

Thought this might resonate with what the team is tackling today.

Best,
Mike
```

After one follow-up, close the loop. If they do not respond, archive the lead and move to the next target. High agency means respecting boundaries and maintaining focus on high-yield opportunities.
