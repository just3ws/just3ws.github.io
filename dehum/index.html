Epistemic Warning & The Comprehension Crisis: The Hallucination Series by Mike Hall

The Comforting Lie of Passing Specs: In legacy software systems, myriad hands over decades push changes to complete tickets in isolation. We run test suites to prove that something "works," but green checkmarks often measure comforting lies rather than physical reality. A passing spec proves only that the code satisfies the assertion you wrote, not that the system is doing what it should, or that anyone left alive still comprehends the trajectory of the system.

Straight to Legacy vs. AI Slop: What's the Difference? Nothing. Nobody really understood what went in there either way. "Straight to legacy" was code hallucinated by a human developer completing a Jira ticket in isolation, satisfying an isolated unit test, and merged as opaque tech debt. "AI slop" is the exact same phenomenon generated in seconds by an LLM, adorned with zero-cost badges and diagrams, and merged without personal accountability.

The LLM Scale & The Strawberry Paradox: Today, millions of developers are pushing AI-generated code into production that they do not fully understand, because true domain comprehension is prohibitively expensive in time and cognitive resources. We find ourselves in an uncanny reality: an LLM cannot reliably count the "R"s in "Strawberry," yet it can construct and reason through a complex 10-harmonic notch filter DSP pipeline.

Where Does Responsibility Land? When tickets were completed by humans in isolation, we shipped slop daily. With generative AI, that disconnect scales exponentially, severed from any individual held accountable. Does the onus land on the person prompting, or is responsibility pushed higher up the org chart into plausible deniability? This repository is an open forensic investigation into that exact razor's edge: probing where human discernment ends, synthetic extrusion begins, and how to verify physical ground truth when comprehension cannot keep pace with generation.
